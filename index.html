🎯 Design Principles & Interaction Design

Design principle Interaction design
Affordances & signifiers Interactive objects use clear outlines, contrasting colours, icons, and short labels. Targets highlight when selected or focused.
Hover & selection feedback Objects change colour or glow when targeted. A selection indicator confirms where the user is aiming.
Action feedback Successful throws, passes, and hits trigger animations, score updates, and optional spatial audio or controller vibration.
Error recovery Missed actions show gentle visual guidance and allow retries without harsh penalties.
Spatial ergonomics Keep menus within a comfortable forward field of view, around eye level, and avoid requiring repeated overhead reaching.
Comfort & fatigue Provide seated play, adjustable session lengths, pause options, and alternatives to large arm movements.
Interaction challenge & iteration If a full-arm swing is tiring or difficult to detect, replace it with a short flick or button-triggered action and test whether it feels easier to control.

🎮 Input Method & Rationale Matrix

Input method How the user interacts Why it was chosen
Hand tracking Pinch, grab, or perform a short hand gesture to interact with objects. Supports natural interaction without requiring complex controller movements.
Controller input Press buttons to shoot, pass, hit, or activate actions. Provides reliable alternatives for users who find gestures difficult.
Gaze targeting Look at a target to aim or highlight an object. Reduces the need for large arm movements.
Seated movement Use a joystick, small lean, or simplified input to move or reposition. Supports users who play from a wheelchair or seated position.
Adaptive triggers Use a tap, hold, or release to perform a sport action. Makes actions adjustable to different physical abilities.
